# Project Description! 

Understanding who influences EU policy, and how much they spend doing it, is really hard to figure out. Lobbying data is technically available to the public, but it’s scattered, confusing, and pretty inaccessible for most people. We are building a web application to address the lack of lobbying transparency by letting users search any policy area and immediately see which organizations are lobbying on it, how much money they are spending, where they’re from, and what industry they represent. 

To make the analysis more meaningful, we’re combining lobbying data from LobbyFacts.eu with World Bank API data from https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/topics/125589, including GDP, population, and government transparency scores, to add economic and political context to the lobbying patterns we’re uncovering. 

Our app is designed for three types of users: investigative journalists following the money, political science researchers looking for patterns, and everyday citizens who just want to understand who is shaping the policies that affect them.




# User Personas

## Tintin
Bio: International Independent Investigative Journalist
A sharp and fiercely independent investigative journalist and reporter who travels the world exposing corruption, political schemes, and works informing citizens on hard facts using raw data. He has recently shifted his focus to Europe, and more specifically Belgium, as he is researching lobbyists and European Politics.

User stories
1. As a journalist, I want to research a policy topic and see a breakdown of top organizations involved, so I can report which sectors are most influential. 

2. As a journalist, I want to build and save custom comparison charts and update them as my investigation evolves, so I can return to my research from any device. 

3. As a journalist, I want to delete saved charts I no longer need and save filter presets for searches I run repeatedly, so my workspace stays organized. 

4. As a journalist, I want to add and update annotations on an organization's profile, so I can track my investigative findings alongside raw data. 

5. As a journalist, I want to export filtered results and ML predictions to Excel or PNG, so I can back my reporting with hard data. 


## Jacques Clouseau:
Bio: A world-class political science researcher
A world-class political science researcher at KU Leuven specializing in EU legislative behavior. Once a celebrated crime detective, Clouseau traded case files for policy files, applying the same obsessive attention to detail to uncovering how lobbying groups shape European law. He doesn't just want to know who is lobbying; he wants to know if it worked. His current research focuses on tracing the pipeline from lobbying expenditure to legislative outcomes, building an evidence base that can withstand academic peer review. He collects data like evidence, cross-references everything, and trusts nothing he can't verify with numbers.

User stories
1. As a researcher, I want to search a policy area and see which organizations have lobbied on it alongside related legislative outcomes, so I can assess whether lobbying actually shaped policy. 

2. As a researcher, I want to compare lobbying spending across organizations and save those comparisons, so I can return to them later. 

3. As a researcher, I want to save and update my filter presets (industry, country, date range), so I can quickly re-run the same queries.

4. As a researcher, I want to add my own supplementary notes to an organization's profile and delete any I find inaccurate, so I can keep my research clean. 

5. As a researcher, I want to correlate an organization's ML influence score with actual legislative outcomes over time, so I can validate which groups are genuinely shaping policy. 

## Stromae:
Bio: A Common European Citizen 
A 19 year old, first-year university student in Leuven who just voted in his first European election. He follows politics loosely through TikTok and Instagram and is curious about how the EU actually works, but the institutional websites feel impenetrable and dry. He has a short attention span for dense text but loves visual explainers and "wait, what?" moments that he can screenshot and send to his group chat. He's not looking for a research tool; he wants to learn enough to feel informed and form opinions.

User stories
1. As a curious newcomer, I want to search and filter by topic or country, so I can explore issues I actually care about.

2. As a curious newcomer, I want to save my favorite topics and filters, so I can quickly return to content I found interesting.

3. As a curious newcomer, I want to create a personal feed by selecting topics I care about and update my preferences as my interests change, so the app surfaces relevant content automatically.

4. As a curious newcomer, I want to bookmark "did you know?" facts and delete ones I no longer care about, so my saved content stays relevant.

5. As a curious newcomer, I want to see influence scores as a simple visual ranking with plain-language tooltips that explain what the numbers mean in everyday terms, so I can understand who matters without feeling overwhelmed by raw data.
